# Gradual introduction and integrated mastery

## Experience promise

Beta begins as an approachable adventure in a living place. The player should understand their immediate intention, available actions, likely costs and a useful next step without studying the simulation. Early scenes need space, readable composition, modest sound and a few consequential choices. The world can already contain politics, food webs, geological history and complex production while the player engages with an ordinary local concern.

The progression destination is coordinated use of the connected systems. By the end game, capabilities, companions, settlements, trade, ecology, magic, history and institutions can reinforce one another. Greater scope should produce informed orchestration rather than a permanently expanding wall of meters and compulsory chores. Personally mastering every craft remains optional: allies, tools, contracts and delegation provide legitimate integration for an adventurer or specialist.

The [research study](../research-v0_9/procedural-onboarding-and-mechanic-pacing.md) supports the teaching pattern and states its limits. Earlier earned-knowledge and difficulty contracts remain in force; this document adds explicit procedural introduction and attention design.

## Introduction is generated content

Maintain an authored teaching graph separate from the world's causal/progression graph. A teaching unit defines the concept, prerequisites, observed affordance, grounded motivation, safe or recoverable practice, independent-use check, later combination, accessible explanation and optional skip/reminder behavior. It names allowable actor/locale/item bindings and valid alternatives. It does not instantiate a disposable teacher, grant powers for viewing a hint or force an NPC to act against its own knowledge and commitments.

During supported-start generation, bind a small initial set of teaching units to the actual world: available food and tools, nearby people, a plausible task, readable terrain, viable routes and suitable time/season. Verify both a constructive gameplay advancement witness and a teaching-opportunity witness. For example, a winter start can use stored food or an appropriate local activity; it cannot demand a fresh summer fruit that cannot be obtained. A flooded bridge may introduce cooperation through a valid repair, safe detour or crossing service according to actual capabilities.

Bound generation retries and retain the accepted binding. If a candidate start cannot supply its selected introduction, choose another compatible authored pattern or regenerate the uncommitted start under the declared profile. Do not publish a broken introduction and patch it by conjuring hidden supplies afterward. Original Alpha content and its supported routes remain required; teaching enriches those situations rather than substituting unrelated demo quests.

## Observe, practice, use and combine

| Stage | Player-facing experience | Generator/director responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | A readable situation suggests an action: ripe produce, a helper using a tool, a neutral animal grazing or a damaged crossing | Bind truthful cues to actual state; reveal only essential information and a practical response |
| Guided practice | Try the action with clear controls, low simultaneous demand and affordable recovery | Supply legitimate inputs/access and a comprehensible result; avoid stacking combat, inventory overload and multiple new systems at once |
| Independent use | Apply the familiar action in a slightly changed situation | Validate the enabling resources and meaningful choice; recognize supported alternatives instead of requiring one scripted gesture |
| Combination | Solve a problem using familiar actions together with one new concept | Rate the composite capability/resource/knowledge/time challenge, and avoid a hidden dependency on another unintroduced system |
| Orchestration | Coordinate people, tools, production, environment and historical opportunities across a larger intention | Use the same commands and facts with clear delegation, progress, interruptions and exception handling |

First minutes and first sessions have human-tested pacing targets, not fixed timers that force tutorials while a player is thinking. Readiness comes from demonstrated use, explicit player preference and relevant acquired knowledge, with accessible reminders. A player who discovers a mechanic early can bypass redundant explanation; a returning player can skip basic controls without receiving unearned in-world class knowledge or resources.

## Few foreground concerns, many background causes

Initially foreground one chosen local intention with a small number of relevant actions. Queue or bundle nonurgent notifications; keep contextual help concise and dismissible. Environmental life remains visible without demanding immediate management: a bird can graze, rain can fall and a distant caravan can move without each producing a new objective or meter. Background mechanics become foreground when they enable a wanted action, create an understandable local consequence or become the player's chosen responsibility.

Separate player-input tutorials from character knowledge and capability. A human may know how inventory works while their new character cannot identify a toxin. Conversely, a skilled companion may possess expertise without obliging the player to read every formula. Persist assistance preferences in the appropriate player/profile context and world learning in the actor's actual state; co-op control handoff and alternate characters cannot silently mix them.

Default panels answer practical questions: what can I do, what will it cost, what is blocking it, and what happened? Earned details can expose process graphs and conditional forecasts. The simple view remains available at every level. Geography, ecology, NPC emotion and sound communicate useful state with restrained cues; more simulated variables do not require more visible overlays.

## Procedural scheduling and changed worlds

The introduction director selects eligible opportunities from the current world and the learner's readiness. It does not own NPC decisions, weather, rewards, encounter outcomes or the simulation clock. Each unit uses actual actor agency, commitments, supplies, time windows and location access. A teaching opportunity or feasibility witness reserves nothing. Only an accepted ordinary task or commitment may reserve resources or an actor, with normal expiry, cancellation and revalidation. Ignoring or skipping guidance cannot sequester a mentor, food or tools; no unlimited tutorial inventory exists alongside the economy.

World changes can interrupt teaching. If a mentor departs, a workshop floods or a creature is hunted, invalidate the binding and seek a truthful compatible continuation: another known helper, a retained book/record, a different practical task, waiting for a recurring opportunity or an openly unavailable route. An existing unit can complete through a legitimate alternate solution. Never resurrect a teacher, fabricate evidence or silently make a player action fail to preserve the lesson.

Supported beginner starts intentionally constrain initial risk and introductory availability through declared generation profiles. They are not guarantees that every voluntary destructive decision remains recoverable. Foreshadowed hard gates may hide in plain sight, but known danger and retreat choices should distinguish them from the immediate introductory path. Catastrophic geology and demanding magical/ecological combinations can exist in world history before requiring the beginner to manage them.

The first ordinary problem should not secretly require learned hydrology, soil chemistry, politics, crafting research and body-equipment management simultaneously. If several systems cause the problem, provide a low-complexity response such as asking an existing specialist or using a familiar tool. Later lessons can reveal why it happened and offer more powerful alternatives.

## Co-op and differing experience

Each player can receive appropriate concise guidance without pausing other players through a new tutorial clock. Shared encounters, rest, dialogue and hazards continue to obey existing host/Wait policies. A veteran can help with tools or demonstrate an action, but a party member's knowledge does not automatically become everyone's secret information. Track individual learned capabilities and agreed shared discoveries explicitly.

Avoid four simultaneous introductions speaking over one another. Shared opportunities identify participants and roles; personal overlays remain private. A late joiner can receive a grounded orientation to the current settlement and recent relevant facts, rather than replaying the seed's original introduction over an altered world. Assistance should summarize what is now true and what is actionable.

## Late-game coordination

Build complexity through combinations of familiar concepts: a crop shortage becomes a supply/irrigation problem; a cave discovery becomes a safe excavation and transport choice; a damaged route becomes a caravan/engineering/political task; a tainted forest becomes containment, ecological recovery and historical investigation. Each step reuses known actions while widening the causal picture.

Coordination interfaces express intentions and constraints. Assign a repair with a material budget, reserve a harvest for a household, ask a caravan to carry seeds, delegate a watch or commission a researched tool. Show responsibility, expected conditions and meaningful exceptions. NPCs execute actual feasible work; delegation cannot bypass costs or force personality/loyalty changes. The player intervenes in consequential choices instead of acknowledging every routine tick.

Optional detailed inspection supports specialists, and endgame scenarios deliberately combine broad systems. Personal mastery of every profession is not the only route to participating: party synergies, institutions and trade carry expertise. The full simulation remains integrated under both a hands-on adventure view and a broader planning view.

## Acceptance and production

P00 records the teaching graph, learner/profile versus character-state split and valid-start constraints. P02 implements one native original-content introduction with observe/practice/use/combination, clear feedback, low simultaneous demands and a small actionable UI. P03 tests alternate solutions, changed world/mentor, season/resources, late join and mixed-experience co-op. P04–P10 expand the same teaching grammar with their actual mechanics. P12 verifies early sessions and late-game orchestration across unfamiliar seeds; P13 closes release evidence and P14 retains mobile accessibility.

LEARN-01–04 in the research dossier and the revision validation register remain unrun. Retain actual human/virtual-build sessions, build/seed/start/knowledge state, prompts shown, choices made, failed actions, help requests, interruption recovery and what participants understood. Do not infer comfort from a low popup count or mastery from clicking through a tutorial. Test whether early play feels spacious and directed, whether discovery remains voluntary, and whether late complexity feels coherent and rewarding.
