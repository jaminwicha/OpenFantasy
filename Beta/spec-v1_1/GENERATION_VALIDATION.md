# Procedural composition validation

## Purpose and commitment boundary

Generation should produce coherent combinations, not place every eligible idea at once. Validate candidate worlds, regions, settlements, adventurelets and encounters before their state becomes authoritative. Repair a bounded local conflict, remove an optional proposal, choose another compatible authored pattern or reject the candidate. A supported new-world setup may offer or automatically try another seed under its disclosed policy. A failing exact seed must be reported as rejected, not silently presented under a different seed label.

This extends the [geological generator](GEOLOGICAL_WORLD_DESIGN.md), [progression/teaching contract](PLAYER_EXPERIENCE_AND_ONBOARDING.md) and [local graph-validation lessons](LOCAL_REUSE_DECISIONS.md). It is a shared validation framework with domain-owned predicates and witnesses, not another runtime state owner or a promise that a generic solver understands all content.

Before commitment, proposals can be changed or discarded without fictional consequences. After commitment—including remote NPC realization before a player visit—terrain, actors, possessions and events are actual world facts. A validator cannot remove an inconvenient dead teacher, reroll a flooded town or replace a discovered deposit. Changed-world validation reports invalidated opportunities and enables legal in-world responses through normal authority. Historical refinement must satisfy already established anchors at all dates; it cannot use a new random seed to erase an uncomfortable past.

## What a proposed feature declares

Each feature family defines stable identity/version, requested space and time, required inputs/actors/roles, supported context, offered capabilities, exclusions, dependencies, resource and workload budgets, observation/privacy effects, and presentation needs. Distinguish a reusable definition from an instance's placement. A profession, deity or vehicle may be required product scope without appearing in every introductory village. Required Alpha content has explicit placement and migration obligations that optional pruning cannot waive.

Use typed relationships where possible: exclusive physical occupancy; shared compatible occupancy; must-connect/must-separate; requires-before; consumes/reserves; same-person versus distinct-person; climatic/habitat suitability; declared magical exception; protected historical anchor; narrative role exclusivity; and optional preference. Record the predicate and affected entities so a conflict is explainable and a minimized failing seed can be retained.

## Layered checks

| Layer | Examples of invalid combinations | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions and budgets | Missing reference, illegal effect recursion, unsupported body/action/audio family, unbounded spawn/work, impossible required package dependency | Compile-time types, finite catalog coverage and declared work bounds |
| Macro geography and history | Neighboring catchments disagree, region has no terminal preparation boundary, river source occurs after its dependent city, moving feature loses identity | Shared macro records, terminating dependency witness, ordered chronology and stable anchors |
| Geometry and topology | Stairs arrive inside solid material, two exclusive structures occupy one volume, city sewers break a foundation, cave cannot connect to its entrance | Realized 3D occupancy/support/navigation checks, not only a room graph |
| Ecology and materials | Ordinary crop has no viable season/resource route, creatures have no supported food source, fluid creates volume, biome label contradicts actual cover | Habitat/resource/process constraints, establishment/food/flow accounting and explicit fantasy exceptions |
| People and institutions | One actor must simultaneously fill mutually exclusive roles, dead actor accepts a living-only job, assigned office lacks required authority, culture/history references disagree | Actor state, calendars, commitments, recognized institutions and knowledge scopes |
| Quest, puzzle and progression | Key behind its own lock, prerequisite teacher unavailable, unfunded reward, clue never observable, time window impossible with required travel/cost | Constructive composite capability/resource/time/plane witness and validated alternatives |
| Attention and repetition | Several identical rescue plots crowd the start, overlapping tutorial prompts demand many unfamiliar systems, repeated sprite/prop placement destroys legibility | Authored uniqueness windows, salience/attention budgets, native composition and human seed sampling |
| Cross-layer continuity | Optional ruin removal deletes a required historical clue, biome repair destroys a trade route, a quest repair exceeds conserved stocks | Dependency invalidation, revalidated witnesses, explicit acceptance report |

An apparent contradiction can be intentional content. Conflicting religious beliefs, a forged document, an unusual individual, a city built over an older crypt or a magical forest in a dry region can be valid when their distinct factual/subjective layers and supported causes are represented. The validator checks that an exception is declared and executable. It must not flatten interesting mysteries or cultures into uniform consistency.

Redundancy likewise needs context. Several houses sharing a regional craft style are coherent; several supposedly unique artifacts using one identity are not. Repeated everyday life is necessary for credible cities. Diversity scoring measures meaningful roles, situations, silhouettes and placement within appropriate windows; it does not ban all repeated textures or require every citizen to have an unrelated unique quest.

## Bounded resolution pipeline

1. Generate proposals from stable address/domain/version inputs against immutable committed context.
2. Validate hard constraints and produce actionable conflict sets with involved claims, witnesses and dependency revisions.
3. Apply deterministic bounded local repair or backtracking using authored alternatives. Keep priorities explicit; CPU scheduling, discovery order and client arrival cannot choose the winner.
4. Prune optional proposals by stable policy, releasing their provisional claims and invalidating dependent proposals once. Revalidate effects on required content, progression, history and attention.
5. If required constraints still fail, reject the candidate region or uncommitted world. Retain seed, generator versions, attempt address, violated predicates and a minimized example when possible.
6. Publish the validated result and its relevant witnesses atomically under the existing authority. Concurrency accepts one consistent result; rejected proposals leave no actor, resource or reservation residue.

Set finite attempts, candidate sizes and solve budgets at each scope. A local repair cannot trigger an endless continent-wide retry chain. Reject unsupported cyclic dependencies or resolve an explicitly finite simultaneous constraint group. Negative results may be cached with version/context keys; a changed definition or historical anchor invalidates the appropriate result. Never reuse a failure cache as evidence against a different world revision.

For new-world creation, bounded automatic reseeding can follow an explicit “find a valid world” choice. Record the accepted actual seed and attempt lineage. A user asking for a particular seed receives that seed's success or an understandable failure, with another candidate available. For an already running world's unexplored frontier, use deterministic compatible local alternatives without changing global seed or committed borders. If no valid continuation fits the required contract, stop publication at the preparation boundary and report the generator defect; do not silently create impossible terrain or a second history.

Selective removal acts on optional feature instances, not the promised game scope. Record why an omitted proposal was optional and what supported alternative or remaining coverage exists. All required mechanics still appear in the release catalog and valid generation families; all required Alpha content remains accounted for. An always-pruned feature is a coverage defect or an explicitly rejected optional candidate, not a successful completed system.

## Runtime validation and quality learning

Once play begins, validate commands and process commits against current versions, occupancy, quantities, actor agency and knowledge. Generation certificates do not permanently guarantee feasibility after an earthquake, murder, embargo or player excavation. Retain historical outcomes and update affected opportunities truthfully. Live hazards revalidate pending stages; do not force an earlier predicted result because its initial proposal once passed.

Automated checks can prove selected invariants and constructive examples, not universal fun or absence of every semantic contradiction. Sample unfamiliar seeds with independent critics, actual-build virtual players and humans. Record clustering, repetition, blocked progression, overwhelming introductions and native readability. Feed diagnosed cases into definition constraints and authored alternatives rather than accumulating opaque ad hoc exclusions.

## Qualification cases and delivery

All cases are unrun Beta obligations. They extend existing GEOR, PLACE, LEARN and LOCAL witnesses; use shared harnesses rather than independent generation engines.

| Case | Required result | Introduction → full integration |
|---|---|---|
| GEN-01 | Deliberately collide stacked structures, aquifers, entrances and reciprocal chunk boundaries; repair or reject deterministically without stale occupancy or source claims | P01 → P07 |
| GEN-02 | Combine an unreachable teacher/key, unfunded reward and impossible seasonal/time window; retain a valid composite path or reject the uncommitted start; optional pruning cannot remove Alpha obligations | P02 → P09 |
| GEN-03 | Give one actor incompatible timed roles and overlap several redundant adventurelets/tutorials; choose a coherent subset with finite retries, no leaked reservations and manageable foreground demands | P02 → P09 |
| GEN-04 | Compare reverse worker/request order, exact-seed failure and permitted reseed; accepted output, actual seed and rejection report remain truthful; historical anchors cannot change | P01 → P10 |
| GEN-05 | Invalidate a previously valid route through committed player/world action; preserve events and actors, offer only legal continuations, and never reroll visited or remotely realized state | P03 → P10 |

P00 establishes predicates, conflict reports, optional/required obligations and retry policies. P01–P03 exercise these counterexamples with retained generators before broad production. P04–P10 expand domain definitions; P11 checks feature families against chronic pruning and content loss; P12 requires long unfamiliar-seed and early/late experience tests; P13/P14 preserve supported-profile and regression evidence. A generator's ability to reject a bad seed is useful only alongside demonstrated production of enough varied, valid and enjoyable worlds within measured preparation budgets.
