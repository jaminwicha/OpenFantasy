# Gameplay, performance and narrative integration

## Shared contract

Every supported action and consequential world state needs a coherent expression in the game. The authority commits facts; knowledge rules determine what an observer can perceive; presentation communicates those permitted facts; players and NPCs respond through legal actions; narrative retains the resulting consequences. Art, animation and prose cannot create an independent inventory, relationship, injury, deadline or historical event.

Each production feature record includes its requirement IDs, authoritative state/effect identifiers, observation conditions, basic feedback, optional learned inspection, available actions, performance variants, interruption rules, narrative hooks and acceptance evidence. A feature is not complete because a menu exists or because a concept image depicts it. Engine builders, artists, writers, audio creators and testers share the same fixture and resulting state.

The owner’s reference targets form two independent acceptance bars: **Sea of Stars-level pixel-art finish** and **Stardew Valley-like responsive animation, graphical response and everyday world interaction**. Review composition, palette, material detail and lighting separately from tool contact, work loops, creature reactions, paired gestures, object changes and interruption. Both must pass in playable native scenes. More effects or finer detail cannot compensate for unavailable actions or unresponsive objects; preserve the accepted restrained hierarchy and accumulated history.

The following matrix establishes minimum coverage. Each row expands into native assets and definitions for every supported shipping family. It does not promise that one sprite clip can represent every body, object, tool or terrain combination.

## Native asset and performance coverage

The accepted direction is restrained 16-bit+ pixel art with Sea of Stars-level finish and rich readable interaction comparable to the desired Stardew-like world. Existing style options and five composed reference scenes remain preserved. Their approval establishes the visual direction and feedback about repetition; it does not establish final native scale, animation coverage or a completed production library.

Each supported actor body declares foot/contact anchors, facing, locomotion, equipment/attachment slots, interaction reach and posture. An action declares tool/prop needs, contact events, legal duration and interruption. The asset manifest maps body × equipment × action × condition × viewing direction to an approved representation or a specifically reviewed supported alternative. No original-content family may ship with a permanently unaccepted placeholder simply because its combination is unusual.

Layered clothing, tools and effects can reduce duplication, but their contacts and silhouettes require native review. A sword grip, knitting needle, child-care action, bird mount and missing arm cannot be assumed compatible through a single generic animation. Use shared motion families where they actually fit; author the important exceptions. Procedural offsets may support gazes, secondary movement or props, while characteristic poses and key actions retain pixel discipline and readable silhouettes.

Variation is semantic and restrained. Use stable family variants, material age, repairs, clothing and meaningful personal features to avoid conspicuous repetition. Do not add random ornament everywhere. Scars and wear reflect actual state where the game treats them as evidence; ambient texture that carries no fact must not be presented as a clue. Crowd layout, spacing and motion should vary without rerolling a person's identity every time a chunk loads.

The camera selects a useful slice of stacked space and preserves interaction clarity. A roof fade or cutaway may reveal a known interior without exposing an undiscovered basement or hidden occupant. Nearby NPCs can perform detailed work while distant people use less frequent or less detailed presentation; their authoritative activity and identity persist. Offscreen simulation does not require rendering every person.

## Choreography and interruption

A performance describes intention, participating identities, blocking, gaze, gestures, prop handling, dialogue/audio, authored beats and permitted interruption. The authority validates required movement and actions before their effects commit. A cinematic animation event can request a command or acknowledge a visual beat; it cannot mint an item, heal a character or alter an affinity directly.

Scene state must distinguish preparation, performing, awaiting a legal action, interrupted, resumed and completed. Track which authoritative effects already happened. Reconnecting cannot replay a reward because a handover animation began again. A dead or departed actor cannot continue speaking merely because a timeline holds a reference. A caravan can stop a conversation because an actual threat was perceived; it need not wait for an unrelated animation track to finish.

Wait preserves cosmetic performance only where it cannot change gameplay. A fluttering banner is harmless; a burning beam about to collapse over participants is a coupled hazard. Puzzle mechanisms, vehicles, pets, dream tethers and external interventions use the same boundary rules. Presentation should make a held encounter understandable without falsely claiming the whole independently exploring world is frozen.

## Five retained integration scenes

These are planned native validation scenes derived from the existing content/reference places. They require actual builds and user review; this document does not claim they have been produced.

1. **Wren, autumn:** a supply disruption changes a merchant's stock, a household's cooking plan and a companion's mood. Gather a substitute, prepare tea, repair a textile and resolve a local conversation or task. Observe weather, meaningful variety and a quiet camp interaction through one coherent style.
2. **Gearford, winter:** inspect a damaged support beneath an occupied workshop, mine and haul an appropriate material, forge or repair a part and change the usable route. Show stacked floors, tool contact, cold exposure, injury adaptation and NPC work resumption.
3. **Willowick, oasis:** a caravan arrives with real goods and tired animals. Feed/care, negotiate or escort, and choose between a risky faster route and a scheduled safer departure. A hot spring and an NPC preference create a social or preparation opportunity without free recovery.
4. **Sunken Crypt, rain:** evidence and route access differ between current ruins, an earlier requested date and a shadow-plane correspondence. Solve through investigation, physical manipulation or a supported combined ability. Rain, old repairs and absent witnesses retain their proper causal meaning.
5. **Brasswing, storm break:** a damaged vehicle and its crew face a local Wait encounter while another player acts elsewhere. Resolve an interruption, a restrained scar or eligible rare injury, repair and a changed political order. Dialogue and body language respond to acquired news and actual outcomes.

## Acceptance and production records

For each scene, retain native screenshots, motion/video evidence, build/version, seed, checkpoint, input sequence, relevant state/event trace and unresolved defects. A human reviews style, clarity, repetition, emotion, comfort and overall interest. Virtual playtesters operate the build and report observed behavior. A critic can identify likely contradictions from the contract but cannot certify motion or enjoyment from prose.

Measure the number of unique supported actions, native exceptions, visible repetitions, unreadable cues and manual authoring effort. Do not treat generated asset count as throughput unless assets pass the actual acceptance and integration steps. Per-domain coverage and the full skill catalog must be complete before production acceptance, even when early scenes demonstrate only representative families. Future expansion uses the same contract and does not bypass approval by calling an asset procedural.
