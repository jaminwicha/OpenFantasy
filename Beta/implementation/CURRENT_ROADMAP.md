## Where the project stands

The project is implementing **P02's inhabited playable loop**, with substantial P01 authority and
some later-domain foundations. P00/P01 have outstanding exit obligations; P02 is not near a justified
phase-close claim. The broad continuing implementation goal is not complete. The next useful
milestone is a coherent, discoverable player journey that consumes the systems already built and
adds the missing character/narrative/environment introductions. More isolated proof packets alone
cannot close it.

At the audit's accepted source boundary, `ffa1f971`, real saved gameplay includes source-backed
gathering, crafting, Wren's funded quest/building, paid commerce and cargo travel, road danger,
Wait/retreat/outcomes/care, authenticated four-human connections and selected-character permissions.
Shared Pack, Recipes, construction, Trade, Care and independent History have bounded native
evidence. Deterministic transactions, original-genesis replay, archive continuity and recovery are
substantial strengths. See [shared History](p02/shared-history/RESULT.md) and its linked predecessors.

The ordinary local launcher still opens the profile5 construction farm. Newer hosted cargo/control
admissions compose many more systems, but these are explicit routes. There are 38 supported snapshot
profile codecs, not 37 completed worlds. Preserving old meanings is necessary; the player-facing
entry has not kept pace. The original farm is one 32×32×16 region; the cargo corridor has 14 such
regions within a 16-region interpretation. Those proofs do not establish an effectively endless
frontier, a 20,000-resident city, or complete NPC cognition.

The [roadmap critic](roadmap-audit-20261003/ROADMAP_CRITIC.md) checks every phase and domain. Its
[requirement matrix](roadmap-audit-20261003/requirement-matrix.json) retains all 181 exact requirement
texts/hashes and introduction/full-completion mappings. This is traceability, not181 completed tests.
No full requirement is mapped to completion in P01–P03; their demanding introduction/proof exits
still apply. The [progress audit](roadmap-audit-20261003/PROGRESS_AND_EFFICIENCY.md) measures activity
and workflow cost without claiming a shipping percentage, date or provider-credit saving.

## Risks and efficiency controls

The hardest unresolved feasibility questions are composed clocks/hazards/history at scale, deep NPCs
with bounded distant work, meaningful procedural advancement, and production throughput for native
content/presentation. They need executable evidence, not another general research round. Finite
catalogs must enumerate all promised families without implying that every imaginable combination is
authored separately. Semantic action kits and validated composition must control the variant matrix.

Recent integration is fast, but no defensible completion percentage or ship date exists. The audit
records 274 Beta-path commits from September 8 through October 3's accepted checkpoint, including fixes
and documentation. Six full Core sweeps repeated11,310 cases in11.4minutes; their actual binary pins
explain the reruns, and this is not the dominant-time claim. Implementation records occupy5.92GB of
logical file contents and132 P02 packets. Preserve evidence, but stop multiplying custom verifiers
and status narratives. See [metrics and interpretation](roadmap-audit-20261003/PROGRESS_AND_EFFICIENCY.md).

Report accepted player journeys/dependencies removed, new defects and open gates over named periods.
Keep new tests separate from regression totals. Classify production, fixture/checker, infrastructure
and unresolved reliability failures. Reuse exact proven dependencies; qualify common runner/artifact
reuse in one bounded tooling unit before relying on it. Never weaken gates or delete old evidence.
Keep one current roadmap and a short handoff; source-based documentation review is part of the same
change. Delegate coherent independent work only when expected benefit exceeds setup/integration
cost; do not claim subscription savings from list-price estimates or native-agent parallelism.
