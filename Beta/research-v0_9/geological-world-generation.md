# Geological world generation and historical continuity

Research checked 8 September 2026. This paper informs the new geological refinement; it does not modify the frozen v1.0 specification. It complements [mutable terrain/runtime research](../research-v0_8/terrain-runtime-and-worlds.md) and the separate [climate, glaciers and underground-water investigation](submerged-subterranean-and-glacial-worlds.md). Its scope is geological cause, terrain generation, historical change and deterministic regional integration. No reference-game binary, proprietary source, simulation benchmark or Beta geological implementation was acquired or executed.

**Recommendation:** create a persistent hierarchy of geological features, material reservoirs, drainage constraints and time-dependent processes, then realize playable terrain from it. Use simplified tectonic and geomorphic models whose consequences persist. Avoid both unrelated noise maps for different eras and a promise to numerically simulate an entire Earth's mantle at gameplay resolution.

## 1. What the named games actually establish

Bay 12 describes Dwarf Fortress as a generated persistent world with civilization history and more than 200 rock/mineral types placed in geological environments. This is primary evidence for geology affecting material availability and world context, not proof of a numerical tectonic history. [Official feature description](https://bay12games.com/dwarves/features.html).

The developer's 13 March 2007 entry records changed rock placement, inclusions, gems and metal veins. The 16–17 March entries distinguish global and intermediate information used for local rivers, tributaries, sources and mouths, including river-loop fixes. These are unusually relevant architectural details: world-scale constraints precede local geography. Later entries distinguish local flow, melting/freezing and terrain support work. They do not establish continuous continental drift or scientific river erosion during ordinary play. [Bay 12 development log, March 2007](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2007.html).

An August 2008 fix explicitly concerns peaks incorrectly raised during erosion. It establishes that an erosion operation exists, but does not specify a current hydraulic solver, conservation model or moving-plate simulation. Historical development notes must not be promoted into a complete description of today's closed engine. [Bay 12, 2 August 2008](https://bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2008.html).

Terraria offers a different precedent. tModLoader's maintained world-generation guide describes a finite two-dimensional tile array and sequential passes composed of steps. It distinguishes creation-time generation from in-game editing and the latter's framing/multiplayer obligations. Its API describes tile/ore runners and seed variants. This is direct evidence from the extension project's maintainers, not publication of Terraria's entire original source. [tModLoader generation guide, revised 25 July 2025](https://github.com/tModLoader/tModLoader/wiki/World-Generation), [WorldGen API](https://docs.tmodloader.net/docs/stable/class_world_gen.html).

The public `WorldGen.cs.patch` adds hooks around the ordered generator, records mod versions used during generation and exposes later Hardmode passes for Good/Evil terrain and walls. Thus terrain can change after initial creation through explicit gameplay progression. That is not evidence of geology evolving through eons. The useful lesson is inspectable, versioned transformation stages; Beta must add history, conservation and causal ownership. Mutable source references require a pinned commit before implementation reuse. [Public tModLoader patch](https://github.com/tModLoader/tModLoader/blob/stable/patches/tModLoader/Terraria/WorldGen.cs.patch).

| Reference evidence | Retain | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| DF regional materials and hierarchical rivers | Geology informs mining; macro geography constrains local maps | Complete tectonic evolution, universally realistic minerals or continuous live erosion |
| Terraria/tModLoader generation and later transformation passes | Bounded stages, content hooks, debugging and version records | Deep geological chronology or an unlimited three-dimensional world |
| Scientific/procedural research below | Selected causal relationships and algorithms | Drop-in deterministic co-op, historical saves or enjoyable catastrophe pacing |

## 2. Scientific causes worth preserving

Continents and mountains should have histories, not merely height thresholds. USGS describes Appalachian rocks assembled from old continents, marine sediments, volcanic deposits and oceanic crust, then transformed by collision. A mountain can therefore expose material deposited in a former sea; an old coast can sit far from today's ocean. This supports coherent stratigraphy, fossils, mining knowledge and exploration across eras without requiring every mineral grain to be simulated. [USGS, Geology of the Southern Appalachian Mountains, 2008](https://www.usgs.gov/maps/geology-southern-appalachian-mountains).

Uplift and erosion operate together. USGS's New York account describes repeated continental collision, deformation/metamorphism, accretion and subsequent erosion/deposition; old mountain-building rocks remain after the relief has changed. Beta should preserve both formation age and later exposure/deformation. “Ancient” must not always mean low, ruined or mineral-depleted; later uplift can expose older formations. [USGS, Geology of the New York Region](https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/geology-new-york-region).

Volcanism is related to tectonic setting, but is not interchangeable with mountain height. USGS describes subduction-linked volcanic arcs and also intraplate hotspot chains. Use distinct source/vent settings and eruption profiles; avoid putting a volcano on every peak or making every earthquake emit lava. Cooling/deposition can create new terrain and material reservoirs with recorded source budgets. Magical/crystal causes may add explicit alternative processes, identified as the world's fictional rules. [USGS, Volcanoes: Plate-Tectonics Theory](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/volc/tectonics.html).

Earthquakes arise from sudden fault slip after stress accumulates; creep and discrete rupture can coexist. Represent accumulated regional state and a specific rupture event rather than rolling unrelated damage for every chunk. Shaking, fault displacement, landslides and water effects are distinct consequences, not mandatory features of every quake. Player knowledge can improve construction and risk assessment without revealing the simulation's future event seed. Real scientific prediction does not provide an exact future earthquake date, location and magnitude. [USGS earthquake causes](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-earthquake-and-what-causes-them-happen), [USGS prediction limits](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/can-you-predict-earthquakes).

River change also has several timescales. USGS's Virgin River investigation documents channel migration, sediment accumulation and avulsion: a blocked/aggraded channel can breach elsewhere and abandon its previous route. Flood sequence and duration mattered, not just a single maximum discharge. Beta can therefore create a stranded port, lost bridge or fertile new floodplain through actual sediment and channel events. A one-off flood should not automatically redraw an entire continent. [USGS Water-Resources Investigations Report 97-4073, geomorphic-process and avulsion sections](https://pubs.usgs.gov/wri/wri974073/report.html).

A sinking city needs an identifiable mechanism. Groundwater extraction can compact susceptible sediments and increase local flood risk; it is not equivalent to a continent disappearing into the sea. Separately, USGS documents regional land-level changes following megathrust earthquakes. Keep land displacement, ocean/lake level and temporary inundation separate, then derive the local shoreline. A drowned town can retain foundations, cargo, ownership claims and surviving residents. Detailed aquifer, ice-load and climate processes belong to the companion investigation. [USGS land subsidence](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/land-subsidence-in-california), [USGS subduction-zone events, 2020](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/subduction-zone-science/science/introduction-subduction-zones-amazing-events).

These sources ground relationships, not game tuning. Extreme events should not be made frequent merely to advertise a system. Long quiet intervals, preparations, minor tremors, repair and historical evidence make rare large changes meaningful.

## 3. Procedural research: useful approximations and limits

**Cortial, Peytavie, Galin and Guérin (2019), Procedural Tectonic Planets**, approximates movement, collision and subduction of continental/oceanic crust, then amplifies coarse results into detailed relief. It explicitly targets controllable graphics rather than the complexity of geological physical models. This supports separating a coarse evolving substrate from fine terrain. Its planet domain and interactive authoring are not proof of online unlimited planar generation or lossless historical reconstruction. The publisher abstract and author-hosted indexed text were available; full-PDF retrieval exceeded this research tool's size limit. [Publisher record, DOI 10.1111/cgf.13614](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.13614), [author-hosted paper](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Articles/2019-planets.pdf).

**Cordonnier et al. (2016), Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion**, combines prescribed uplift with a stream graph. Its governing relation balances uplift against an erosion term depending on drainage area and slope: `dh/dt = u − k A^m s^n`. This makes watershed connectivity consequential instead of carving arbitrary cracks into noise. The paper discusses geological scales of roughly 10^5–10^7 years and tens to hundreds of kilometres: that relation is not a direct per-frame flood-damage rule. Adopt its coupled-process idea, then qualify an integer approximation and material accounting. Available publisher/indexed paper text supported this review; full-PDF retrieval exceeded the tool limit. [Paper, DOI 10.1111/cgf.12820](https://cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonier16CGF.pdf).

**Génevaux et al. (2013), Terrain Generation Using Procedural Models Based on Hydrology**, first builds a hierarchical drainage graph over an input domain, analyzes watersheds and river types, and combines terrain/river primitives through a construction tree. Its explicit limitation is terrain shaped by hydraulic erosion; it is not a complete generator for every volcanic, glacial or fresh geological surface. Its continuous detail representation is also different from Beta's mutable discrete cells. Retain drainage-first constraint propagation and feature hierarchy, not a claim that one algorithm covers all landforms. The full ten-page paper was accessible. [ACM paper, DOI 10.1145/2461912.2461996](https://cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Genevaux13ToG.pdf).

**CHONK 1.0 (2024)** combines topographic graphs, water/sediment transfer and labeled material provenance. Its distinction between passively routing through depressions and explicitly representing lake volumes is valuable. It also exposes a determinism trap: its numerical treatment perturbs elevations to resolve equal-height ambiguities. Beta needs canonical tie rules and versioned arithmetic, not fresh unrecorded noise. The authors discuss complexity and performance tradeoffs instead of claiming their more elaborate lake treatment is universally faster. Borrow sediment labels, explicit storage and directed dependencies while keeping the local fluid solver's responsibilities separate. [Geoscientific Model Development paper](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/71/2024/gmd-17-71-2024.html).

No research prototype above was ported or benchmarked. Their floating-point implementations and modeling errors cannot silently become Beta's cross-platform rules.

## 4. Recommended hierarchy for Beta

The following is a design inference, not an observed reference-game implementation:

1. **Geological history:** stable crust/plate/terrane, fault, vent, formation and basin identities; versioned births, splits, collisions, uplift/subsidence intervals, deposition and disruptions. Separate feature identity from location at a given instant.
2. **Regional constraints:** basement lithology, strata relationships, deformation fields, relief envelopes, drainage divides, outlet elevations and finite contributing catchments. Neighboring regions share the same boundary records.
3. **Landform realization:** valleys, ridges, terraces, cones, beaches and sediment bodies derive from those constraints plus stable detail seeds. Glacial inputs alter this substrate through the companion climate/ice contract.
4. **Playable cells:** quantize the resulting volumes into the existing discrete topology and material model, then apply persistent mutations. Construction, excavation and lived erosion change this state; reloading cannot restore a regenerated baseline over a player's mine.

A fixed world cell address is not a persistent piece of crust. Geological displacement and erosion can move or destroy a material parcel while a city/formation keeps its historical identity. Record transformations and provenance, not only a new heightmap. A time-travel query may find that the same address was underwater or inside rock; travel preflight must handle the actual destination using the game's admission/capability rules instead of changing geology to make it convenient.

**The almost-unbounded planar frontier is the difficult boundary.** The cited algorithms operate over supplied finite domains or planets. A chunk-local height function cannot in general know its complete upstream drainage or future neighbors' tectonic obligations. Beta should commit a deterministic macro hierarchy with finite, closed contributing catchments and known outlet or endorheic storage contracts before fine terrain. Cross-region rivers use shared ports carrying elevation, direction, source identity and time-dependent flux obligations. Large basins can span many regions; their contributing set must still be finite and progressively resolvable.

The hierarchy must bound ancestor/dependency discovery and impose canonical tie-breaking. Do not disguise square generation seams as mandatory coastlines, wrap an unlimited plane into a hidden small sphere, or revise an already known river because the neighboring region was visited later. A later dam, avulsion or quake may legitimately change routing through a recorded event, with downstream dependencies updated; mere realization order may not. This is a falsifiable proposed architecture, not a claim that unlimited globally coupled geophysics is solved.

## 5. Ancient history, lived events and consequences

Generated prehistory should store causal process intervals and discrete events. A requested intermediate instant evaluates the declared versioned interval law and ordered events at that instant; it must not interpolate two unrelated pictures. Coarse geological fidelity is acceptable when explicit and consistent with established facts. Actual lived changes require retained commands/events/deltas, including partial erosion, transported material and unfinished jobs. Neither reversing erosion nor regenerating with today's package can reconstruct the past.

Pin the historical head/cursor and feature versions as the existing history service requires. Refinement at one ancient date constrains later/earlier queries without producing different geology from query order. A playable historical edit forks a child with inherited material claims and changed subsequent causes. It does not rewrite another player's source history or promise the same future after a changed river.

Erosion, sediment transport, redeposition and volcanic input require reservoir accounting. A grain need not be an entity, but a material quantity cannot appear in two recoverable ore bodies without declared creation/transformation. Preserve source intervals through splitting, mixing and re-exposure; otherwise a geological event could launder branch-export duplication. Crust/deep-source generation budgets must be distinguished from ordinary renewable production.

Lived geology operates through ordinary damage, support, fluid, navigation, economy and NPC systems. A flood that changes a trade route can interrupt caravans, prompt evacuation, shift prices and generate repair work. Inundation does not delete the town's population ledger. Geological changes must also invalidate dependent vertical volumes and paths consistently, including unloaded neighbors.

Large hazards need finite propagation/support footprints and resumable work. Couple a hazard front to an encounter before it affects held participants; do not allow unseen catch-up harm. Conversely, sharing a distant ocean or plate identity cannot automatically freeze an entire planet for one decision. Macro reservoir/transfer contracts and bounded causal arrival fronts separate remote dependencies from immediate contact. Unsupported couplings need the project's explicit fallback policy and proof of progress, not discarded consequences. Detailed numerical thresholds belong in the new specification and qualification fixtures.

## 6. Retained falsification and feasibility cases

All cases below are **unrun**. Retain their seeds, rules, event prefixes, request order, expected state/provenance and actual performance evidence.

| Case | Required proof |
|---|---|
| GEOR-01 — causal chronology | Reconstruct a basin before deposition, after burial/uplift and after erosion, including arbitrary interior instants. Query in reverse order and after restart; formations, exposed materials and known facts agree. |
| GEOR-02 — frontier boundaries | Realize opposite sides of a plate boundary, watershed divide and river outlet in different orders/worker counts. Verify equal shared contracts, finite dependency discovery and no retrospective river/ore movement from loading. |
| GEOR-03 — conserved transformation | Erode and transport a mineral-bearing formation, redeposit it, excavate both eras and attempt cross-branch transfer. Account for remaining reservoirs, transformed quantities and duplicate claims through crash/retry. |
| GEOR-04 — event integration | Apply earthquake displacement or volcanic deposition to lived terrain containing a mine, occupied stacked town, ship and held encounter. Verify complete support/hazard/custody consequences, finite work and ordinary NPC responses. |
| GEOR-05 — river/coast change | Compare gradual migration, avulsion, a dam and subsidence. Preserve distinct land/water states, sediment budgets, old channels and submerged structures; update real navigation/trade rather than only artwork. |
| GEOR-06 — historical scale and play | Prepare large geological intervals and several active regions while normal play continues. Measure storage growth, preparation latency, backlog recovery and knowledge-safe forecasts; human review assesses whether change creates meaningful play rather than routine destruction. |

The research supports a coherent, simplified historical geology architecture. Its unresolved proof obligations are deterministic regional closure, process resolution, historical storage growth and engaging event frequency—not whether every moment must run a planetary physics solver.
