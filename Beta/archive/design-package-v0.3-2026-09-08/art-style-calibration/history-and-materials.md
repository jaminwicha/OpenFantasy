# History visible in materials

Owner direction, 8 September 2026: the world carries accumulated eons, and individual objects may show decades or centuries. The atmosphere comes from use, adaptation and remembered ownership as well as visual beauty. The Star Wars comparison refers to that sense of a used, inhabited world, not its recognizable designs or fictional technology.

**Detail should explain a life.** A newer repair over an old repair is often more evocative than another ornamental sparkle. Preserve the inviting, colorful pixel-art direction while adding distinct generations of workmanship. This is not an instruction to make everything dirty, broken or ancient.

**Apply detail judiciously.** The owner's review of v05 endorsed the ideas but found a little too much sensory density: “less is more.” Preserve craftsmanship and the strongest historical cues while reducing repeated ornaments, glowing marks, flowers and isolated texture flecks. Give paths, ordinary surfaces and character silhouettes quiet surroundings. Concentrate richness at a few meaningful focal points; do not make every object and surface compete for attention. Restraint changes the distribution of detail, not the quality standard.

## Art brief

| Category | Details that carry history | Keep readable |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Older foundations under newer walls; reused dressed stone; blocked former doorway; differently aged mortar; roof patches and replaced beams | Structural silhouette, current entrance, walkable edge and elevation |
| Paths and bridges | Hollowed steps; polished thresholds; repaired parapet; replaced planks; wheel grooves and changing route alignments | Traversal route, hazards, interactive structures and shorelines |
| Metalwork | Patina in recesses; bright handles from contact; changed fasteners; mismatched replacement gear; worn maker's stamp | Working surfaces and mechanisms; metal does not become an undifferentiated brown mass |
| Ceramics and domestic objects | Mended cracks, worn glaze, altered lids, family marks, a repair in a later style | Object identity and purpose; small props retain a few strong clusters |
| Tools, weapons and clothing | Rehandled tool; old blade in a newer grip; sewn patch; inherited clasp; restitched hem; softened leather | Character silhouette, profession, equipment and animation consistency |
| Landscape and creatures | Roots around abandoned masonry; weathering across exposed strata; lichen patterns; an older shell with healed wear; plants adapting around old waterworks | Habitat, creature identity and biological variety; age is not universal decay |
| Magic | An old ward incorporated into a newer lintel; mineral glow in worn channels; faded ritual marks; a tended shrine with replacement offerings | Magic belongs to specific objects or processes; avoid ambient glitter concealing the material story |

Use several timescales in the same scene. Geological forms can imply deep time. Foundations, water routes and ruins can span civilizations. A building or inherited tool can span generations. Today's swept doorstep, fresh board or polished kettle shows that people still care for the place. A recently made object should look recently made, even in a very old world.

Visual history should vary by material, environment and use. Water erodes differently from foot traffic; exposed wood ages differently from a sheltered chest; a cherished brass handle may shine while its recesses tarnish. Choose a few coherent causes and express them with purposeful pixel clusters. More colors, micro-noise or overlays do not automatically produce more atmosphere.

This is an evolving world. The [environment and maintenance design](/Users/alucinor/Workspace/Projects/OpenFantasy/Beta/environment-aging-and-maintenance.md) covers daily light, moving clouds, weather, wind, seasonal variants, cumulative exposure and functional repairs. ## Connection to the simulation

The proposed core distinguishes definition, object instance, ownership lineage, material, manufacture, condition and consequential repair/modification events. Renderers can select authored variants or controlled overlays from that state. A fixed style seed supplies harmless visual variation; current condition and significant history supply meaningful changes.

Do not procedurally redraw every sprite after every tick. Start with artist-approved age/repair states for a few representative assets and explicit attachment/decal points where useful. Keep history data engine-neutral and presentation selections in the asset package. Test that the variants still animate and tile cleanly.

Separate three kinds of detail:

1. **Cosmetic age cues:** provide atmosphere without promising a discoverable quest. They must be plausible for the material and approximate age.
2. **Inspectable evidence:** marks, repairs or inscriptions backed by actual world facts, a maker, an owner, or a documented uncertainty. A description cannot invent a named previous owner that the simulation does not contain.
3. **Functional condition:** damage, upkeep and modifications that affect performance through the shared item/building rules. Visual wear and functional danger must not contradict each other without an intentional explanation.

An inherited tool can thus look old, belong to a known family, bear a later repair and work well because it has been maintained. That object can connect an original OpenFantasy quest, a RogueFiction-like discovery and a household's daily work without requiring a separate story-only inventory item.

## Calibration and review

Keep earlier art options unchanged. Evaluate the next refinement against the same village, travelers, reed-shell creature and prop subjects, emphasizing visible material history and reducing decorative magic wherever it competes with that history. The exact degree of detail remains a user choice.

Review first at gameplay scale: does the place feel inhabited across generations, do current residents still belong there, and can the player still read the route and objects? Enlarged inspection then checks purposeful pixels, repeatable variants and distinct materials. A concept can establish atmosphere; production acceptance still requires native assets and motion samples.
